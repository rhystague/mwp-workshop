# Process Free Writing

## Overview

Treat free writing as idea discovery before writing polish. Preserve the researcher's phrasing and intellectual agency while converting exploratory material into claims, tensions, concepts, map-note updates, and possible paper seeds.

## Vault Resources

Read these only when the task reaches the relevant step:

- `Researcher Identity.md`: use when judging fit with the researcher's frame of Accessible Spatial Interaction and Nonvisual Navigation.
- `Research Questions.md`: use when linking claims, tensions, or paper seeds to live questions.
- `agents/templates/Map Note Template.md`: use before creating or substantially restructuring a map note.
- `agents/templates/Potential Paper Template.md`: use before creating a potential paper note.
- Relevant notes in `Free-Writing/`, `Maps/`, `Paper-Notes/`, or `Potential-Papers/`: read when the user points to them or when a proposed update depends on existing context.

Do not invent citations, findings, quotations, page numbers, methods, paper records, or evidence. Mark unsupported claims and evidence needs directly.

## Workflow

### 1. Establish The Source

Identify the source material:

- A pasted free-writing excerpt.
- A note in `Free-Writing/`.
- A rough research reflection, navigation observation, or idea dump.
- A cluster of fragments from several notes.

Ask for the missing note or excerpt only when no workable source material is available. Otherwise proceed and state the evidence basis briefly.

### 2. Preserve Useful Language

Extract useful phrases, sentences, or short passages before summarizing. Keep language that shows:

- A vivid claim or formulation.
- A recurring tension.
- A possible concept name.
- A methodological or theoretical intuition.
- A sentence that could anchor a paper, map note, or reflection.

Do not over-polish. Lightly clean obvious transcription issues only when clarity improves without changing the voice.

### 3. Extract Research Material

Convert the free writing into compact working material:

1. Candidate claims.
2. Tensions or contradictions.
3. Concepts and possible Obsidian wikilinks.
4. Research questions.
5. Evidence needs or missing methods.
6. Connections to existing papers, maps, paper ideas, or research strategies.

Distinguish the researcher's expressed idea from assistant interpretation. Flag overclaims, unsupported causal claims, vague novelty, and concepts that need sharper boundaries.

### 4. Connect To The Research Frame

Use the vault's default research identity as the anchor:

- Core identity: Accessible Spatial Interaction and Nonvisual Navigation.
- Current context: blind and low-vision adults using mobile, audio, and tactile interfaces in public buildings, transport interchanges, and connecting routes.
- Contribution: route comprehension, orientation, attention, recovery, sensory preferences, and sustained interface use.

Mark material as adjacent unless it clearly connects back to nonvisual spatial representation, sensory interaction, orientation, or navigation for blind and low-vision people.

### 5. Suggest Map And Paper Updates

Suggest map-note updates only when the insight is likely to recur or help synthesis. For each suggestion, name:

- Target map note, existing or proposed.
- Distilled insight to add.
- Why the update matters.

Suggest potential paper ideas only when the material has a plausible problem, gap, question, or contribution. Keep each paper seed small enough to test. If the user asks to save one, read `agents/templates/Potential Paper Template.md` before creating or updating `Potential-Papers/`.

### 6. Propose A Micro-Outline Last

Offer a micro-outline only after exploratory value has been captured. The outline should help the researcher continue writing without locking the idea too early.

Use this shape when helpful:

1. Opening problem or provocation.
2. Core tension.
3. Working claim.
4. Evidence or examples needed.
5. Next paragraph or next small writing move.

## Output Standards

Prefer concise working academic prose over polished essay prose. Keep the output useful for future synthesis and writing, not administratively heavy.

When updating files, preserve useful phrasing from the source, add Obsidian wikilinks where they aid retrieval, and keep uncertainty visible.
