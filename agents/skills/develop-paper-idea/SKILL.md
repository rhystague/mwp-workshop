# Develop Paper Idea

## Overview

Turn early research material into a plausible, scoped paper candidate anchored in the researcher's identity: Accessible Spatial Interaction and Nonvisual Navigation.

Prefer concise, working academic prose over generic brainstorming. Preserve the researcher's intellectual agency by distinguishing the user's idea, assistant interpretation, and possible paper argument.

## Vault Resources

Read these only when needed:

- `Researcher Identity.md`: use as the anchor for fit, positioning, and scholarly voice.
- `Research Questions.md`: use to connect the idea to live questions and existing paper seeds.
- `agents/templates/Potential Paper Template.md`: use before creating or updating files in `Potential-Papers/`.
- Relevant notes in `Free-Writing/`, `Maps/`, `Paper-Notes/`, or `Potential-Papers/`: read when the user points to them or when the idea depends on nearby vault context.

Do not invent citations, findings, DOIs, quotations, page numbers, empirical evidence, or venues. Mark uncertain evidence needs clearly.

## Workflow

### 1. Establish The Seed

Identify the available source material:

- A rough idea from the user.
- A free-writing note or excerpt.
- A map-note cluster.
- A paper seed from `Research Questions.md`.
- An accessibility, navigation, audio-interface, or tactile-interaction observation.

Ask for missing context only when the seed is too vague to scope responsibly. Otherwise proceed and state any assumptions briefly.

### 2. Anchor The Idea

Use the research identity as the default frame:

- Core identity: Accessible Spatial Interaction and Nonvisual Navigation.
- Current context: blind and low-vision adults using mobile, audio, and tactile interfaces in public buildings, transport interchanges, and connecting routes.
- Contribution: understanding how the form and timing of spatial information affect route comprehension, orientation, attention, recovery, and sustained interface use.

Mark topics as adjacent unless they clearly connect back to nonvisual spatial representation, sensory interaction, orientation, or navigation for blind and low-vision people.

### 3. Shape The Candidate

Produce the following, adapting labels to the user's request:

1. Sharper working title.
2. Problem and why it matters now.
3. Research gap.
4. One main research question.
5. Possible working claim.
6. Contribution type: conceptual, empirical, methodological, practical, design, or mixed.
7. Evidence needed.
8. Likely audience or venue.
9. Risks or weak points.
10. Next small action.

If the idea is too broad, split it into 2-4 smaller paper candidates and identify the most actionable one.

### 4. Improve Scholarly Usefulness

Pressure-test the candidate:

- Flag overclaims, weak novelty, missing methods, unclear evidence, causal leaps, and scope problems.
- Prefer evidenceable questions over broad position statements.
- Convert generic accessible-navigation claims into specific claims about spatial comprehension, cue interpretation, attention, orientation, recovery, or sustained interface use.
- Suggest only concrete next actions that would reduce uncertainty or move writing forward.

### 5. Create Or Update A Potential Paper Note

When the user asks to save, create, update, or develop a vault note:

1. Read `agents/templates/Potential Paper Template.md`.
2. Create or update a concise note in `Potential-Papers/`.
3. Use the working title as the filename unless the user specifies another name.
4. Preserve useful phrasing from the source material.
5. Add Obsidian wikilinks where they aid retrieval, especially `[[Researcher Identity]]` and relevant concepts or maps.
6. Keep status honest: seed, developing, outlining, drafting, or parked.

Do not create heavy administration. The note should be useful for future paper development, not a template-filling exercise.

## Output Standards

Keep outputs actionable and compact. Use the researcher's scholarly frame, but do not rewrite their identity or imply a firm commitment where the idea is still exploratory.

When evidence is thin, say so directly and identify what would make the paper viable.
